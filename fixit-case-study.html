01 — Problem

When informal becomes unacceptable

Nigeria's urban rental market runs on WhatsApp, verbal agreements, and scattered bank alerts. This informality isn't inconvenient — it's the root cause of financial disputes, unresolved repairs, and broken trust.

01

No paper trail

Rent payments are confirmed via WhatsApp screenshots or bank alerts — easily deleted, disputed, or ignored. Tenants have no centralized payment history.

02

Maintenance in limbo

Issues are reported via text messages that get buried. No tracking, no timelines, no accountability. Tenants often self-repair at personal cost because waiting is worse.

03

Escalating disputes

Without documented evidence, deposit conflicts and reimbursement claims become "he said, she said" situations with no resolution path — only eroded trust.

There is no structured system for tenants and landlords in urban Nigeria to document rent transactions, track maintenance requests, or maintain verifiable accountability — leaving both parties financially and emotionally exposed.

Key Findings

01

WhatsApp is the default record system — but it's fragile. Participants described forwarding screenshots to prove payment, yet landlords sometimes claimed non-receipt. One interviewee said: "The agent shows you the property and then disappears."

02

Tenants self-fix to avoid delays — Tunde Adeyemi, our self-reliant persona, had spent ₦42,000 out of pocket on an electrical repair because the landlord wouldn't respond in time for his family's safety.

03

Responsibility is ambiguous — Multiple participants said they weren't sure if repairs were the landlord's or tenant's responsibility. "Responsibilities should be clearly written in the agreement."

04

Documentation gaps create fear — Zainab Musa saves every receipt but still feels unprotected: "Ensure that you keep the receipt of payment. Don't pay cash."

05

Payment timing creates anxiety — Participants described the dread of receiving bank alerts that showed "no confirmation" from the landlord side for days. Payment transparency is a safety need, not a convenience.

Key Insight
The emotional low point wasn't during repair delays — it was the moment of uncertainty: not knowing if anyone had received the message.
User Journey Insight

Mapping Tunde's journey from Issue Happens → Outcome (No Accountability) revealed the deepest emotional dip occurred at the "Trying to Get a Response" phase — not during the repair itself. This reframed the design challenge: it's not just about tracking, it's about eliminating the anxiety of not knowing.

How Might We Questions

Generated from the Opportunities row of the journey map, these questions became the design brief:

HMW 01 …ensure rent payments are transparent and verifiable so tenants and landlords always have proof?
HMW 02 …make maintenance requests trackable and actionable so issues don't fall through the cracks?
HMW 03 …reduce disputes around reimbursements so tenants don't self-finance landlord responsibilities?
HMW 04 …improve landlord responsiveness and accountability without requiring confrontation?
HMW 05 …centralise tenant-landlord communication so nothing important is ever "just a WhatsApp message"?

05 — Design Decisions

Every screen has a reason

I prioritized four features using a MoSCoW framework — not because they were easy, but because research showed they addressed the most critical pain at the deepest emotional moments.

📋

Role-Based Onboarding

A Select Role screen (Tenant / Landlord) allows the same app to serve two distinct user types with different workflows, dashboards, and permissions — without needing two separate apps.

Why this decision?Research revealed that landlords and tenants have fundamentally different mental models and actions. A unified role switch avoids fragmentation and reduces development complexity.
🔢

Unique Request IDs (REQ-XXXX)

Every maintenance submission generates a traceable ID. This transforms an informal complaint into a formal, reference-able record — creating psychological accountability for both parties.

Why this decision?3 of 5 participants cited "not having a reference" as a key reason disputes escalate. A visible ID changes the expectation: this will be tracked.
📊

Status Timeline (Not Just Status)

Request Information shows a vertical timeline: Submitted → In Progress → Completed with timestamps. This directly addresses the "trying to get a response" emotional low point in the journey map.

Why this decision?The emotional pain wasn't the delay itself — it was the silence. A timestamped timeline eliminates the uncertainty that causes anxiety, even when progress is slow.
📸

Photo Evidence Upload

The Request Description screen allows optional photo uploads — transforming a verbal complaint into documented, timestamped visual evidence. This supports reimbursement claims and prevents landlord-tenant disputes.

Why this decision?Affinity mapping showed photos were already being sent via WhatsApp — users had the instinct. FixIt channels that behavior into a permanent, structured record.
🏠

Property Code Setup

Tenants link their account to a property using a unique code or address — automatically associating all activity with a specific tenancy. This makes switching between properties seamless for future use.

Why this decision?Users in compounds often have multiple shared concerns. Property-based grouping allows landlords to see all requests per property and prevents cross-tenancy confusion.
⚡

4-Tab Navigation Architecture

Home · Requests · Logs · Profile. I considered adding Payments to the primary nav, but after reviewing persona priorities, Logs (the audit trail) was more critical for trust than a dedicated Payments tab.

Why this decision?Based on Zainab's persona — her primary need was a searchable, permanent log. During early wireframe feedback, "where do I find old requests?" was the most frequent question.

07 — Feature Prioritisation

Must have. Should have. Could have.

I used a MoSCoW framework to prioritise features. Every "Must Have" was backed by at least 3 participant mentions or a direct link to a journey map pain point.

HMW 01

Payment Transparency System

  • Digital payment receipts (auto-generated) — MUST
  • Payment status tracking (pending / confirmed) — MUST
  • Shared payment ledger (tenant + landlord visibility) — MUST
  • Payment history dashboard — SHOULD
  • Dispute reporting for payments — SHOULD

HMW 02

Maintenance Tracking System

  • Issue reporting with form + media upload — MUST
  • Status tracking (submitted → in progress → resolved) — MUST
  • Time-stamped logs — MUST
  • Landlord notifications — SHOULD
  • Maintenance history archive — COULD

HMW 03

Reimbursement Management

  • Reimbursement request (pre-approval flow) — MUST
  • Receipt upload — MUST
  • Approval / rejection system — SHOULD
  • Reimbursement status tracking — SHOULD
  • Expense log visible to both parties — COULD

HMW 04 + 05

Response Accountability + Communication Hub

  • Automated reminders for pending actions — MUST
  • Response time tracking with timestamps — SHOULD
  • In-app messaging (centralised) — MUST
  • Context-linked chats (payments, maintenance) — SHOULD
  • Escalation option for unresolved issues — COULD

Did I actually solve the problem?

I tested the prototype with 5 participants — asking them to complete the core maintenance reporting flow without guidance. Here is what the data showed and what I changed as a result.

What changed — before and after

Observation Before After Why I changed it
Photo upload visibility Small text link below description Prominent camera icon with visible + button 2 of 5 users scrolled past the upload option. Visual affordance was missing — users had the instinct to add a photo but couldn't find where.
Dashboard information hierarchy Completed requests shown first Active Requests shown as primary card During testing, users said "I want to see what still needs attention first." Completed items are past-facing; active items require present action.
Request confirmation feedback Generic "Submitted" text only Bold REQ-2026-001 ID + Track Request CTA Users reported feeling uncertain whether the submission had "really gone through." A unique ID creates instant psychological closure — this is now real and trackable.
Navbar structure 5 icons incl. Payments as primary nav 4 icons: Home · Requests · Logs · Profile Feedback revealed "Logs" (the audit trail) was more critical to the core use case. Payments moved to Logs section. Reducing to 4 items improved thumb-reach and reduced cognitive load.
09 — Reflection

What I learned by doing the work

Assumption → Reality

I assumed the core problem was payment tracking

After user interviews, I discovered the real problem was more emotional than functional: people felt unheard. The maintenance silence — not the delay — was the true pain. This shifted my design focus toward communication transparency, not just feature checklists.

What I'd Do Differently

I would prototype earlier — with less fidelity

I spent significant time on visual polish before testing core flows. Next time I would bring paper or low-fi wireframes to users in week 6-7 to validate information architecture before committing to high fidelity. Testing reveals what polish cannot.

What Surprised Me

The journey map was the most impactful deliverable

I initially treated the user journey map as a required deliverable. But when I saw Tunde's emotional curve dip sharply at "Trying to Get a Response" — not during the repair — it completely reframed my feature priorities. The journey map was where the real design decisions were made.

Constraint Learned

Designing for two user types in one app is hard

The landlord experience — approving requests, viewing all properties, managing vendors — is a significantly different product to the tenant experience. I scoped to the tenant side for this sprint. A full version would need a separate landlord onboarding flow and dashboard architecture.

If I had more time, I would build next:

Next 01

Landlord Portal

Full property management dashboard — assign vendors, approve reimbursements, view all active requests across properties.

Next 02

In-App Payment

Direct rent payment with auto-generated digital receipts and shared ledger — addressing Zainab's core need for verifiable payment history.

Next 03

Landlord Rating System

Post-tenancy accountability layer — tenants rate responsiveness and maintenance quality, creating public-facing trust signals in the ecosystem.

Next 04

UX Audit as Case Study 2

The YouTube Music audit from Week 3 has potential as a second portfolio case study — redesigning the search and library experience using what I've learned.
